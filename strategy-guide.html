Backtester Parameter Reference

Detailed breakdown of every field, parameter, and configuration option in the Options Backtester — labeled to match the backtester form sections

1/14

Backtest Name

Backtest Name
A descriptive label to identify this backtest in your results list. Use meaningful names like "SPY 0DTE Put Spread March 2025" so you can quickly find and compare results later.
Tip: Include strategy type, symbol, and date range in the name for easy identification.
2/14

Symbol

Symbol
The ticker of the underlying asset to backtest (e.g., SPY, QQQ, AAPL, SPX). The backtester fetches historical price data and options chain data for this symbol. Index options (SPX, NDX) and ETF options (SPY, QQQ) are both supported.
Free tier is limited to SPY, GLD, and QQQ. Standard tier supports all stocks and ETFs. Premium tier is unrestricted.
3/14

Date Range

Start Date & End Date
The time window for the backtest. The engine scans each trading day in this range looking for entry opportunities at the specified entry time. Longer ranges produce more trades and more statistically significant results.
Use at least 3-6 months of data for meaningful results. Free tier is limited to 1 year; Standard to 3 years.
4/14

Entry Time

Entry Time (Start)
The time of day (ET) when the backtester attempts to enter trades. On each trading day, the engine checks conditions at this exact time. Common values: 9:31 (market open), 10:00 (after initial volatility settles), 15:45 (late day 0DTE).
Entry Time End (Optional)
If set, creates a window between Start and End times. The engine looks for the first minute within this window where all entry conditions are met. If conditions aren't met by the End time, that day is skipped. Useful for condition-based entries that might trigger at different times.
Leave the end time blank to enter at a fixed time each day. Use a window when combining with underlying price conditions.
5/14

Underlying Price Conditions

Optional filters that must be true for a trade to be entered. The engine checks these conditions against the underlying's price data at entry time.

None
No entry conditions. The backtester enters a trade every day at the specified entry time (default behavior).
Preset Conditions
Quick-select common conditions without building custom logic:
  • Premarket Change % — Percentage change from previous close to premarket price. Use to filter days with big overnight moves.
  • Change % — Intraday percentage change from the open to the current price at entry time.
  • Gap % — Percentage gap between the previous close and today's open. Positive = gap up, negative = gap down.
  • Change-Open % — Percentage change from the open price. Similar to Change % but anchored to the opening price.
  • Velocity — Rate of price change over a specified number of minutes. Measures momentum speed, not just direction.
Example: "Gap % > 1.0" only enters trades on days with a gap up of at least 1%.
Custom Builder
Build complex conditions using technical indicators. Each row defines a rule:
  • Left Side (Metric) — The value to test: Current Price, SMA, EMA, RSI, or MACD.
  • Day — Which day's data to use: Today, Yesterday, 2 Days Ago, etc.
  • Candle Type — Bar timeframe: 1-minute, 5-minute, 15-minute, hourly, or daily candles.
  • Period/Window — Lookback period for the indicator (e.g., 20 for SMA20, 14 for RSI14).
  • Operator — Comparison: >, <, >=, <=, ==.
  • Right Side (Comparator) — Compare against a fixed value or another indicator (e.g., "SMA 20 > SMA 50").
  • Threshold — Optional offset in dollars or percent (e.g., "Price > SMA 20 + 1%").
Multiple conditions are AND-combined. All must be true for a trade to trigger. Example: "RSI < 30 AND Price > SMA 200" for oversold bounces in an uptrend.
Example 1: Price > 1% Above 9 EMA (5-min bars)
Enter a trade only when the current price is at least 1% above the 9-period EMA on 5-minute candles — a momentum breakout filter.
Builder Configuration:
Metric: EMA Period: 9 Candle: 5-min Day: Today
< (less than) Compare: Current Price Threshold: -1%
Reads as: "Enter when 9 EMA (5-min) is at least 1% below the current price" — i.e., price has pulled away above the EMA by 1%+, confirming upward momentum.
Example 2: Current Price > 1hr Close by 0.5%
Enter only when the current price is at least 0.5% above the most recent 1-hour candle's close — confirming short-term bullish continuation.
Builder Configuration:
Metric: Price Candle: 1-hour Series: Close Day: Today
< (less than) Compare: Current Price Threshold: -0.5%
Reads as: "Enter when 1-hour close is at least 0.5% below the current price" — confirming that price is accelerating above the last hourly bar's close.
6/14

DTE & Strategy Type

DTE
How many days until the option expires. The backtester selects the expiration date that is exactly this many calendar days from the entry date. Enter 0 for same-day (0DTE) expiration — the most popular for scalping strategies.
For calendar/diagonal spreads, this field is replaced by per-leg DTE settings in the leg configuration. Each leg gets its own DTE.
Strategy Selection
Choose from 22 predefined options strategies. The strategy determines which legs are created, their directions (long/short), and types (call/put). See the Strategy Guide tab for detailed explanations of each.
  • Single Leg — Long Call, Long Put, Naked Short Call, Naked Short Put
  • Vertical Spreads — Short Put Spread, Short Call Spread, Long Call Spread, Long Put Spread
  • Iron Strategies — Short/Long Iron Condor, Short/Long Iron Butterfly
  • Straddles & Strangles — Long/Short Straddle, Long/Short Strangle
  • Calendar & Diagonal — Calendar Call/Put, Diagonal Call/Put, Double Calendar, Double Diagonal
6.4

Implied Volatility Filter

IV% Entry Condition
When enabled, the backtester checks the at-the-money implied volatility before entering a trade. Trades are only taken when IV meets your threshold. This lets you filter for high-volatility or low-volatility environments.
  • Operator — Greater than (>), Less than (<), etc.
  • Threshold — The IV percentage level (e.g., 25 means 25% annualized IV).
Use "IV > 30" for credit strategies (sell premium when IV is high). Use "IV < 20" for debit strategies (buy when premiums are cheap).
6.5

Wing Configuration

Allow Skewed Wings
Only appears for Iron Condor and Iron Butterfly strategies. When set to "No" (default), both the put spread and call spread must have equal width. When set to "Yes", each wing can be configured independently for asymmetric positioning.
Skewed wings let you position the iron condor with a directional lean — wider on the side you expect less movement.
7/14

Leg Configuration

Each leg defines one option contract in your strategy. The leg configuration dynamically adjusts based on the selected strategy. Each leg has a strike selection method that determines how the strike price is chosen.

Strike Selection Methods
Six methods are available to choose the strike price for each leg:
  • Mid-Price Range — Select strikes within a minimum and maximum option premium range (e.g., $1.00 to $3.00). Finds the strike whose option premium falls within your specified range.
  • % Distance from Underlying — Select strike as a percentage away from the underlying price (e.g., 2% OTM). Positive = OTM, negative = ITM.
  • $ Distance from Underlying — Select strike at a fixed dollar distance from the underlying (e.g., $5 OTM).
  • $ Distance from Another Leg — Select strike relative to another leg's resolved strike. Used for defining spread width (e.g., "Leg 2 = Leg 1 strike + $5").
  • % Distance from Another Leg — Same concept but using percentage distance from another leg's strike.
  • Delta — Select the strike closest to a target delta value (e.g., 0.30 delta call). The most precise method for consistent positioning relative to probability.
Per-Leg DTE (Calendar & Diagonal only)
For calendar and diagonal strategies, each leg has its own DTE field. The near-term (short) leg uses a shorter DTE, and the far-term (long) leg uses a longer DTE. This creates the time spread that the strategy relies on.
Example: Calendar Call Spread with near leg DTE=7 and far leg DTE=30 sells a 1-week call and buys a 1-month call at the same strike.
Fallback Behavior
If the exact strike matching your criteria isn't available, the backtester uses a fallback: it selects the closest available strike. This ensures the backtest doesn't skip days unnecessarily due to strike availability gaps.
7.5

Net Premium Filter

Min / Max Net Premium
Filters trades by the net premium of the entire position at entry. For credit strategies, this is the total credit received. For debit strategies, this is the total debit paid. Trades outside this range are skipped.
Example: Min=$0.50, Max=$2.00 only takes short put spreads that collect between $0.50 and $2.00 in credit per contract.
8/14

Take Profit

Take Profit (TP)
The profit target for each trade. When the position's unrealized P&L reaches this level, the trade is automatically closed for a profit. Can be specified as a percentage of premium or a fixed dollar amount per contract.
  • Percentage Mode — Closes when profit reaches X% of the premium received/paid. Example: 50% TP on a $2.00 credit spread closes at $1.00 profit.
  • Dollar Mode — Closes when profit reaches $X per contract. Example: $100 TP closes when 1 contract is up $100.
For credit strategies, 50% of max profit is a common target. For debit strategies, 100% of premium paid is typical.
9/14

Stop Loss

Stop Loss (SL)
The maximum loss you're willing to accept on each trade. When the position's unrealized P&L drops to this level, the trade is automatically closed to limit losses. Can be percentage or dollar-based.
  • Percentage Mode — Closes when loss reaches X% of the premium received/paid. Example: 100% SL on a $2.00 credit spread closes at $2.00 loss.
  • Dollar Mode — Closes when loss reaches $X per contract. Example: $200 SL closes when 1 contract is down $200.
A common rule: set SL at 2x the TP for credit strategies (e.g., 50% TP with 100% SL). For 0DTE, tighter stops (50-75%) are common.
10/14

Exit Conditions

Optional signal-based exit rules that work alongside TP/SL. If an exit condition triggers, the trade closes regardless of current P&L. The earliest trigger wins — TP, SL, or exit condition.

Preset Exit Conditions
Same presets as entry conditions (Change %, Gap %, Velocity, etc.) but applied to close positions. Example: exit a straddle if the stock moves more than 3% in either direction.
Custom Exit Builder
Build technical indicator-based exit rules using the same custom builder interface as entry conditions. Full access to SMA, EMA, RSI, MACD comparisons. Example: close a long call if RSI > 70 (overbought signal).
11/14

Concurrent Trades

Allow Concurrent Trades
Controls whether the backtester can open a new trade while a previous trade is still open. When set to "No" (default), only one position can be active at a time — a new trade won't open until the current one closes. When set to "Yes", multiple positions can overlap.
Keep "No" for simpler analysis and realistic capital usage. Use "Yes" for high-frequency strategies like 0DTE where you might enter multiple trades per day.
12/14

PDT Avoidance

Avoid Pattern Day Trader (PDT)
When enabled, limits the number of same-day round-trip trades to comply with FINRA's Pattern Day Trader rule (no more than 3 day trades in a rolling 5-business-day period for accounts under $25,000). Set to "No" if your account is above $25K or if testing with a cash account.
13/14

Starting Capital

Starting Capital
The initial account balance for the backtest. This determines how many contracts you can trade and affects position sizing calculations. Default is $50,000. Results metrics like Return % and Max Drawdown % are calculated relative to this starting balance.
Use your actual trading account size for realistic results. A $5,000 account will produce very different results than a $100,000 account due to position sizing constraints.
14/14

Allocation Per Trade

How much of your account to risk on each trade. Three methods are available:

Percentage of Account
Allocates a percentage of the current account balance to each trade. As the account grows, position sizes grow proportionally (compounding). Example: 5% of a $50,000 account = $2,500 per trade.
Fixed Number of Contracts
Always trades the same number of contracts regardless of account size. Simple and predictable. Example: Always trade 10 contracts.
Fixed Dollar Amount
Allocates a fixed dollar amount per trade regardless of account balance. The number of contracts is calculated by dividing the fixed amount by the trade cost. Example: Always allocate $5,000 per trade.
Percentage-based sizing is the most realistic — it compounds gains and reduces size after losses, mimicking real trading behavior.

Results & Metrics Reference

Complete visual guide to every section, metric, chart, and data field on the Options Backtest Result Detail page

Report Header

The header bar at the top of every result page. Contains the report title, strategy name, and three action buttons:

  • Back to Results — Returns to the results list.
  • Use Template — Copies all backtest settings back into the backtester form so you can re-run with tweaks.
  • Download CSV — Exports the complete trade log (all 39+ columns, all trades) for external analysis.

Equity Curve & Performance Statistics

The equity curve (left) plots cumulative P&L after each trade. Blue line = profitable, red line = losing. The performance statistics panel (right) shows 8 key metrics at a glance: Win Rate, Return %, Profit Factor, Max Drawdown, Avg Trade, and Final Capital.

Look at the curve shape, not just the final number. A $10K profit with a -$15K drawdown is very different from $10K with a -$2K drawdown. Profit Factor > 1.3 and Max Drawdown < 20% are signs of a viable strategy.

Configuration Summary

A read-only summary of all parameters used for this backtest: strategy, symbol, date range, entry time, DTE, capital, position sizing, TP/SL, premium filters, detection interval, PDT rules, and per-leg strike selection. Click Use Template to load these settings back into the backtester form.

Trade Log

A paginated table (10 trades per page) showing every individual trade. Each row is one complete trade from entry to exit. P&L values are color-coded green (profit) or red (loss). The table scrolls horizontally to reveal all columns. The CSV download includes everything.

Entry Information (4 columns)
  • entry_date — Date the trade was opened (e.g., 2026-03-02).
  • entry_time — Time of entry in ET (e.g., 10:00).
  • entry_timestamp — Unix timestamp in milliseconds for precise timing.
  • underlying_price — Underlying price at entry (e.g., 682.95).
Exit Information (4 columns)
  • exit_date — Date the trade was closed.
  • exit_time — Time of exit in ET (e.g., 15:53).
  • exit_timestamp — Unix timestamp in milliseconds for precise timing.
  • underlying_exit_price — Underlying price at exit. Compare to entry price to see stock movement during the trade.
Trade Details (7 columns)
  • strategy — Options strategy used (e.g., Short Put Spread).
  • num_contracts — Number of contracts traded, based on position sizing and max risk.
  • net_premium_entry — Net premium per contract at entry. Positive = credit received, Negative = debit paid.
  • net_premium_exit — Net premium at exit. For profitable credit trades, approaches 0.
  • max_risk — Maximum loss per contract. For spreads = strike width minus net premium.
  • pnl — Total dollar P&L for this trade. Green = profit, Red = loss.
  • exit_reason — TAKE_PROFIT, STOP_LOSS, EXPIRATION, or EOD.
Time Metrics (2 columns)
  • dte — Days to Expiration at entry. 0 = same-day (0DTE).
  • dit — Days in Trade. For 0DTE, typically a fraction (e.g., 0.2 = ~5 hours).
Capital Tracking (2 columns)
  • capital_before — Account balance before this trade.
  • capital_after — Account balance after close. = capital_before + pnl.
Leg-Specific Columns (10 per leg)
Repeated for each leg: leg1_*, leg2_*, leg3_*, leg4_* (Iron Condors/Butterflies use 4 legs).
  • legN_symbol — Full OCC contract symbol (e.g., O:SPY260302P00682000).
  • legN_name — Leg name from config (e.g., "Short Put").
  • legN_strike — Strike price (e.g., 682.00).
  • legN_entry_price — Option premium at entry.
  • legN_exit_price — Option premium at exit.
  • legN_iv — Implied volatility at entry.
  • legN_delta — Delta at entry. Negative for puts, positive for calls.
  • legN_gamma — Gamma at entry. Rate of delta change.
  • legN_theta — Theta at entry. Daily time decay (negative = loses value).
  • legN_vega — Vega at entry. Sensitivity to 1% IV change.
A 2-leg strategy has 39 total columns. A 4-leg strategy (Iron Condor) has 59 columns. The Greeks columns (delta, gamma, theta, vega) are invaluable for understanding why specific trades won or lost.

Decision Tree Log Book

A chronological, day-by-day record of what the backtester did on each trading day. Shows not only which trades were taken, but also why trades were skipped. Each day is a collapsible row with a status badge (Entry/Exit/Skipped) and P&L. Click to expand and see the full decision flow.

Event Types
The expanded day view shows a vertical timeline of events. Each event has a distinct type, icon, and color:
NO DATA
No market data available for this day (weekend, holiday, or data gap).
CONDITIONS NOT MET
Entry conditions were checked but not satisfied. The reason shows the specific condition that failed, including the actual vs. required values:
  • Preset conditions — e.g., "Premarket Change 0.50% failed > 1%", "Gap -2.10% failed > 0%", "Change % 0.42% failed > 1.00%"
  • Custom conditions — e.g., "price 450.20 < SMA20 455.00", "RSI 45.2 failed < 30", "Missing RSI data"
  • Time window — e.g., "Conditions not met in range 10:00-10:30"
These specific failure reasons help you tune your conditions. If you see "Change % 0.42% failed > 1.00%" on many days, consider lowering your threshold.
CONDITIONS MET
All entry conditions passed. Shows the price and time when they were satisfied. The backtester will now attempt to enter a trade.
TRADE SKIPPED
Conditions were met but the trade could not be executed. The reason explains why:
  • Unable to fetch valid option data for strikes — No options contracts available at the target strikes/DTE.
  • No common timestamps across all option legs — Leg data doesn't align for a simultaneous fill.
  • Insufficient capital — Account balance too low for required margin.
  • PDT restriction — Pattern Day Trader rule blocking the trade.
  • Concurrent trade limit reached — Another position is already open.
  • Premium outside filter range — Net premium doesn't meet min/max filter.
  • IV entry condition not met — IV% filter threshold not satisfied (e.g., "IV 18.5% failed > 25%").
ENTRY
Trade successfully opened. Shows: number of contracts, net premium, max risk, expiration date, and per-leg details (position, type, strike, entry price).
EXIT
Trade closed. Shows trade number, exit time, exit premium, and P&L (color-coded). Exit reason sub-icons:
  • STOP_LOSS — Loss limit hit.
  • TAKE_PROFIT — Profit target reached.
  • EXPIRATION — Options expired.
  • EOD — Closed at market close.
ERROR
An error occurred during processing. Shows the error description (e.g., missing data, API timeout, invalid option chain).
Why the Decision Tree Matters
In most backtests, skipped days far outnumber traded days. The decision tree answers: "What happened on the days you didn't trade?"
  • Tune conditions — Too many "CONDITIONS NOT MET" days? Your thresholds may be too strict.
  • Debug data gaps — "NO DATA" entries reveal missing market data days.
  • Validate logic — Are entries triggered at the right conditions and times?
  • Understand skips — PDT, capital, or option data issues blocking trades?

Interpreting Your Results

Signs of a Good Strategy
  • Profit Factor > 1.3 — Earning $1.30+ for every $1 lost.
  • Max Drawdown < 20% — Worst period is manageable.
  • 30+ trades — Statistically significant sample size.
  • Smooth equity curve — Consistent returns, not a few lucky trades.
Red Flags
  • Profit Factor < 1.0 — Strategy loses money.
  • High win rate (>90%) + low profit factor — Small wins erased by rare large losses.
  • Fewer than 15 trades — Could be random chance.
  • Equity curve driven by 1-2 big trades — Not repeatable.
Backtest Limitations
  • Slippage — Real fills may be worse, especially for illiquid options.
  • Commissions — Not included. Factor $0.50-$1.00 per contract per leg.
  • Liquidity — Wide bid-ask spreads on illiquid options reduce real profits.
  • Regime changes — What worked in 2023 may not work in 2025.
