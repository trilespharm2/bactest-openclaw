Backtester Parameter Reference

Detailed breakdown of every field, parameter, and configuration option — labeled to match the backtester form sections

1/14

Backtest Name

Backtest Name
A descriptive label to identify this backtest in your results list. Use meaningful names like "SPY 0DTE Put Spread March 2025" so you can quickly find and compare results later.
Tip: Include strategy type, symbol, and date range in the name for easy identification.
2/14

Symbol

Symbol
The ticker of the underlying asset to backtest (e.g., SPY, QQQ, AAPL, SPX). The backtester fetches historical price data and options chain data for this symbol. Index options (SPX, NDX) and ETF options (SPY, QQQ) are both supported.
Free tier is limited to SPY, GLD, and QQQ. Standard tier supports all stocks and ETFs. Premium tier is unrestricted.
3/14

Date Range

Start Date & End Date
The time window for the backtest. The engine scans each trading day in this range looking for entry opportunities at the specified entry time. Longer ranges produce more trades and more statistically significant results.
Use at least 3-6 months of data for meaningful results. Free tier is limited to 1 year; Standard to 3 years.
4/14

Entry Time

Entry Time (Start)
The time of day (ET) when the backtester attempts to enter trades. On each trading day, the engine checks conditions at this exact time. Common values: 9:31 (market open), 10:00 (after initial volatility settles), 15:45 (late day 0DTE).
Entry Time End (Optional)
If set, creates a window between Start and End times. The engine looks for the first minute within this window where all entry conditions are met. If conditions aren't met by the End time, that day is skipped. Useful for condition-based entries that might trigger at different times.
Leave the end time blank to enter at a fixed time each day. Use a window when combining with underlying price conditions.
5/14

Underlying Price Conditions

Optional filters that must be true for a trade to be entered. The engine checks these conditions against the underlying's price data at entry time.

None
No entry conditions. The backtester enters a trade every day at the specified entry time (default behavior).
Preset Conditions
Quick-select common conditions without building custom logic:
  • Premarket Change % — Percentage change from previous close to premarket price. Use to filter days with big overnight moves.
  • Change % — Intraday percentage change from the open to the current price at entry time.
  • Gap % — Percentage gap between the previous close and today's open. Positive = gap up, negative = gap down.
  • Change-Open % — Percentage change from the open price. Similar to Change % but anchored to the opening price.
  • Velocity — Rate of price change over a specified number of minutes. Measures momentum speed, not just direction.
Example: "Gap % > 1.0" only enters trades on days with a gap up of at least 1%.
Custom Builder
Build complex conditions using technical indicators. Each row defines a rule:
  • Left Side (Metric) — The value to test: Current Price, SMA, EMA, RSI, or MACD.
  • Day — Which day's data to use: Today, Yesterday, 2 Days Ago, etc.
  • Candle Type — Bar timeframe: 1-minute, 5-minute, 15-minute, hourly, or daily candles.
  • Period/Window — Lookback period for the indicator (e.g., 20 for SMA20, 14 for RSI14).
  • Operator — Comparison: >, <, >=, <=, ==.
  • Right Side (Comparator) — Compare against a fixed value or another indicator (e.g., "SMA 20 > SMA 50").
  • Threshold — Optional offset in dollars or percent (e.g., "Price > SMA 20 + 1%").
Multiple conditions are AND-combined. All must be true for a trade to trigger. Example: "RSI < 30 AND Price > SMA 200" for oversold bounces in an uptrend.
Example 1: Price > 1% Above 9 EMA (5-min bars)
Enter a trade only when the current price is at least 1% above the 9-period EMA on 5-minute candles — a momentum breakout filter.
Builder Configuration:
Metric: EMA Period: 9 Candle: 5-min Day: Today
< (less than) Compare: Current Price Threshold: -1%
Reads as: "Enter when 9 EMA (5-min) is at least 1% below the current price" — i.e., price has pulled away above the EMA by 1%+, confirming upward momentum.
Example 2: Current Price > 1hr Close by 0.5%
Enter only when the current price is at least 0.5% above the most recent 1-hour candle's close — confirming short-term bullish continuation.
Builder Configuration:
Metric: Price Candle: 1-hour Series: Close Day: Today
< (less than) Compare: Current Price Threshold: -0.5%
Reads as: "Enter when 1-hour close is at least 0.5% below the current price" — confirming that price is accelerating above the last hourly bar's close.
6/14

DTE & Strategy Type

DTE
How many days until the option expires. The backtester selects the expiration date that is exactly this many calendar days from the entry date. Enter 0 for same-day (0DTE) expiration — the most popular for scalping strategies.
For calendar/diagonal spreads, this field is replaced by per-leg DTE settings in the leg configuration. Each leg gets its own DTE.
Strategy Selection
Choose from 22 predefined options strategies. The strategy determines which legs are created, their directions (long/short), and types (call/put). See the Strategy Guide tab for detailed explanations of each.
  • Single Leg — Long Call, Long Put, Naked Short Call, Naked Short Put
  • Vertical Spreads — Short Put Spread, Short Call Spread, Long Call Spread, Long Put Spread
  • Iron Strategies — Short/Long Iron Condor, Short/Long Iron Butterfly
  • Straddles & Strangles — Long/Short Straddle, Long/Short Strangle
  • Calendar & Diagonal — Calendar Call/Put, Diagonal Call/Put, Double Calendar, Double Diagonal
6.4

Implied Volatility Filter

IV% Entry Condition
When enabled, the backtester checks the at-the-money implied volatility before entering a trade. Trades are only taken when IV meets your threshold. This lets you filter for high-volatility or low-volatility environments.
  • Operator — Greater than (>), Less than (<), etc.
  • Threshold — The IV percentage level (e.g., 25 means 25% annualized IV).
Use "IV > 30" for credit strategies (sell premium when IV is high). Use "IV < 20" for debit strategies (buy when premiums are cheap).
6.5

Wing Configuration

Allow Skewed Wings
Only appears for Iron Condor and Iron Butterfly strategies. When set to "No" (default), both the put spread and call spread must have equal width. When set to "Yes", each wing can be configured independently for asymmetric positioning.
Skewed wings let you position the iron condor with a directional lean — wider on the side you expect less movement.
7/14

Leg Configuration

Each leg defines one option contract in your strategy. The leg configuration dynamically adjusts based on the selected strategy. Each leg has a strike selection method that determines how the strike price is chosen.

Strike Selection Methods
Six methods are available to choose the strike price for each leg:
  • Mid-Price Range — Select strikes within a minimum and maximum option premium range (e.g., $1.00 to $3.00). Finds the strike whose option premium falls within your specified range.
  • % Distance from Underlying — Select strike as a percentage away from the underlying price (e.g., 2% OTM). Positive = OTM, negative = ITM.
  • $ Distance from Underlying — Select strike at a fixed dollar distance from the underlying (e.g., $5 OTM).
  • $ Distance from Another Leg — Select strike relative to another leg's resolved strike. Used for defining spread width (e.g., "Leg 2 = Leg 1 strike + $5").
  • % Distance from Another Leg — Same concept but using percentage distance from another leg's strike.
  • Delta — Select the strike closest to a target delta value (e.g., 0.30 delta call). The most precise method for consistent positioning relative to probability.
Per-Leg DTE (Calendar & Diagonal only)
For calendar and diagonal strategies, each leg has its own DTE field. The near-term (short) leg uses a shorter DTE, and the far-term (long) leg uses a longer DTE. This creates the time spread that the strategy relies on.
Example: Calendar Call Spread with near leg DTE=7 and far leg DTE=30 sells a 1-week call and buys a 1-month call at the same strike.
Fallback Behavior
If the exact strike matching your criteria isn't available, the backtester uses a fallback: it selects the closest available strike. This ensures the backtest doesn't skip days unnecessarily due to strike availability gaps.
7.5

Net Premium Filter

Min / Max Net Premium
Filters trades by the net premium of the entire position at entry. For credit strategies, this is the total credit received. For debit strategies, this is the total debit paid. Trades outside this range are skipped.
Example: Min=$0.50, Max=$2.00 only takes short put spreads that collect between $0.50 and $2.00 in credit per contract.
8/14

Take Profit

Take Profit (TP)
The profit target for each trade. When the position's unrealized P&L reaches this level, the trade is automatically closed for a profit. Can be specified as a percentage of premium or a fixed dollar amount per contract.
  • Percentage Mode — Closes when profit reaches X% of the premium received/paid. Example: 50% TP on a $2.00 credit spread closes at $1.00 profit.
  • Dollar Mode — Closes when profit reaches $X per contract. Example: $100 TP closes when 1 contract is up $100.
For credit strategies, 50% of max profit is a common target. For debit strategies, 100% of premium paid is typical.
9/14

Stop Loss

Stop Loss (SL)
The maximum loss you're willing to accept on each trade. When the position's unrealized P&L drops to this level, the trade is automatically closed to limit losses. Can be percentage or dollar-based.
  • Percentage Mode — Closes when loss reaches X% of the premium received/paid. Example: 100% SL on a $2.00 credit spread closes at $2.00 loss.
  • Dollar Mode — Closes when loss reaches $X per contract. Example: $200 SL closes when 1 contract is down $200.
A common rule: set SL at 2x the TP for credit strategies (e.g., 50% TP with 100% SL). For 0DTE, tighter stops (50-75%) are common.
10/14

Exit Conditions

Optional signal-based exit rules that work alongside TP/SL. If an exit condition triggers, the trade closes regardless of current P&L. The earliest trigger wins — TP, SL, or exit condition.

Preset Exit Conditions
Same presets as entry conditions (Change %, Gap %, Velocity, etc.) but applied to close positions. Example: exit a straddle if the stock moves more than 3% in either direction.
Custom Exit Builder
Build technical indicator-based exit rules using the same custom builder interface as entry conditions. Full access to SMA, EMA, RSI, MACD comparisons. Example: close a long call if RSI > 70 (overbought signal).
11/14

Concurrent Trades

Allow Concurrent Trades
Controls whether the backtester can open a new trade while a previous trade is still open. When set to "No" (default), only one position can be active at a time — a new trade won't open until the current one closes. When set to "Yes", multiple positions can overlap.
Keep "No" for simpler analysis and realistic capital usage. Use "Yes" for high-frequency strategies like 0DTE where you might enter multiple trades per day.
12/14

PDT Avoidance

Avoid Pattern Day Trader (PDT)
When enabled, limits the number of same-day round-trip trades to comply with FINRA's Pattern Day Trader rule (no more than 3 day trades in a rolling 5-business-day period for accounts under $25,000). Set to "No" if your account is above $25K or if testing with a cash account.
13/14

Starting Capital

Starting Capital
The initial account balance for the backtest. This determines how many contracts you can trade and affects position sizing calculations. Default is $50,000. Results metrics like Return % and Max Drawdown % are calculated relative to this starting balance.
Use your actual trading account size for realistic results. A $5,000 account will produce very different results than a $100,000 account due to position sizing constraints.
14/14

Allocation Per Trade

How much of your account to risk on each trade. Three methods are available:

Percentage of Account
Allocates a percentage of the current account balance to each trade. As the account grows, position sizes grow proportionally (compounding). Example: 5% of a $50,000 account = $2,500 per trade.
Fixed Number of Contracts
Always trades the same number of contracts regardless of account size. Simple and predictable. Example: Always trade 10 contracts.
Fixed Dollar Amount
Allocates a fixed dollar amount per trade regardless of account balance. The number of contracts is calculated by dividing the fixed amount by the trade cost. Example: Always allocate $5,000 per trade.
Percentage-based sizing is the most realistic — it compounds gains and reduces size after losses, mimicking real trading behavior.
1/7

Backtest Name

Backtest Name
A descriptive label to identify this backtest in your results list. Use meaningful names like "SPY Gap Up 5% March 2025" so you can quickly find and compare results later.
Include the strategy type, symbol, and condition in the name for easy identification.
2/7

Symbol & Date Range

Symbol Mode
Choose how to specify which stocks to backtest:
  • Single — Enter one ticker symbol (e.g., SPY, AAPL).
  • Multiple — Enter comma-separated tickers (e.g., AAPL, MSFT, TSLA) or upload a CSV file with one symbol per row.
Multiple mode runs the same strategy across all symbols — great for screening which tickers work best with your setup.
Symbol
The ticker symbol(s) to backtest. Supports any US stock or ETF (e.g., SPY, QQQ, AAPL, TSLA). In Single mode, enter one symbol. In Multiple mode, enter comma-separated symbols or upload a CSV.
CSV Upload (Multiple mode)
Upload a CSV file with one ticker symbol per row. The backtester will run your strategy against every symbol in the file. Useful for testing across an entire watchlist or sector.
Start Date / End Date
The date range for the backtest. The engine fetches intraday price data for each trading day in this range and evaluates your entry/exit conditions bar by bar.
Longer date ranges produce more trades and more statistically significant results. Start with at least 1-3 months.
3/7

Entry Conditions

Condition Type
Choose between Preset signals or the Custom Builder:
  • Preset — Quick setup using built-in market signals.
  • Custom Builder — Build complex conditions by comparing technical indicators (SMA, EMA, RSI, MACD) across different timeframes and offsets.
Preset Entry Signals
Five built-in market signals you can configure with an operator and threshold:
  • Premarket Change % — Percentage change from previous close to premarket price. Captures overnight momentum.
  • Change % — Intraday percentage change from previous close. Measures overall daily momentum.
  • Gap % — Percentage gap between previous close and market open. Detects gap-up or gap-down scenarios.
  • Change-Open % — Percentage change from today's open price. Measures movement since the market opened.
  • Velocity (Rate of Change) — Rate of price change over a specified minute interval. Detects rapid momentum bursts (e.g., "price rose 1.5% in the last 5 minutes").
Custom Condition Builder
Build sophisticated entry rules by comparing candle data and technical indicators. Each condition compares a left-side value to a right-side value using an operator.
  • Metrics — Price, SMA (Simple Moving Average), EMA (Exponential Moving Average), RSI (Relative Strength Index), MACD.
  • Timeframes — Minute, Hour, Day candles.
  • Offsets — Compare current vs. prior candles (e.g., "Current RSI vs. RSI 5 bars ago").
  • Operators — >, <, >=, <=, =
Example: "Enter when Current Price > SMA(20) AND RSI < 30" — this finds oversold stocks trading above their 20-day moving average.
4/7

Entry Criteria (Direction & Sizing)

Direction
Whether the strategy goes Long (buy, profit when price rises) or Short (sell, profit when price falls).
Position Sizing
How to determine the number of shares per trade. Three modes available:
  • Fixed Shares — Always trade a specific number of shares (e.g., 100 shares per trade). Simplest option.
  • Fixed Dollars — Allocate a fixed dollar amount per trade (e.g., $5,000). Shares are calculated from the entry price.
  • % of Capital — Allocate a percentage of your current account balance per trade (e.g., 10%). Requires setting a Starting Capital amount. This compounds gains and reduces exposure after losses.
% of Capital is the most realistic mode — it simulates compounding and proper position sizing. Fixed Shares is best for simple comparison across symbols.
Starting Capital (% of Capital mode)
Your simulated starting account balance. Only required when using "% of Capital" sizing mode. The backtester tracks your running balance, compounding gains and reducing size after losses.
5/7

Exit Criteria (TP / SL / Max Days)

Take Profit
The profit target at which positions are automatically closed. Can be set as a percentage gain or a fixed dollar amount per share.
  • Percent — Close when the price rises X% from entry (e.g., 5% gain).
  • Dollar — Close when the price rises $X from entry (e.g., $2.50 per share).
Stop Loss
The maximum acceptable loss per trade before the position is automatically closed.
  • Percent — Close when the price drops X% from entry (e.g., 3% loss).
  • Dollar — Close when the price drops $X from entry (e.g., $1.00 per share).
Max Days in Trade
Auto-close the position after this many trading days regardless of profit or loss. Prevents positions from being held indefinitely. If neither TP nor SL is hit within this window, the trade exits at the closing price on the final day.
6/7

Signal-Based Exit Conditions (optional)

An optional layer of exit logic that can close positions early based on market signals — independent of TP/SL/Max Days. The same Preset and Custom Builder options are available as in Entry Conditions.

Exit Signal
Choose a Preset signal or Custom Builder condition. When the signal triggers on any bar while a position is open, the trade is closed immediately. This overrides TP/SL if it fires first.
  • Preset signals — Same 5 options: Premarket Change %, Change %, Gap %, Change-Open %, Velocity.
  • Custom Builder — Same indicator comparisons: Price, SMA, EMA, RSI, MACD across timeframes.
Example: "Exit when RSI > 70" — closes positions when the stock becomes overbought, even if TP/SL hasn't been hit.
7/7

Advanced Options (optional)

Allow Consecutive Trades
When enabled, the backtester can open a new position in the same symbol even while a previous position is still active. When disabled (default), the backtester waits for the current position to close before taking new entries.
Enabling consecutive trades can dramatically increase trade count but also increases risk. Start with it disabled to evaluate your base strategy.

A paginated table (10 trades per page) showing every individual trade. Each row is one complete trade from entry to exit. P&L values are color-coded green (profit) or red (loss). The table scrolls horizontally to reveal all columns. The CSV download includes everything.

Entry Information (4 columns)
  • entry_date — Date the trade was opened (e.g., 2026-03-02).
  • entry_time — Time of entry in ET (e.g., 10:00).
  • entry_timestamp — Unix timestamp in milliseconds for precise timing.
  • underlying_price — Underlying price at entry (e.g., 682.95).
Exit Information (4 columns)
  • exit_date — Date the trade was closed.
  • exit_time — Time of exit in ET (e.g., 15:53).
  • exit_timestamp — Unix timestamp in milliseconds for precise timing.
  • underlying_exit_price — Underlying price at exit. Compare to entry price to see stock movement during the trade.
Trade Details (7 columns)
  • strategy — Options strategy used (e.g., Short Put Spread).
  • num_contracts — Number of contracts traded, based on position sizing and max risk.
  • net_premium_entry — Net premium per contract at entry. Positive = credit received, Negative = debit paid.
  • net_premium_exit — Net premium at exit. For profitable credit trades, approaches 0.
  • max_risk — Maximum loss per contract. For spreads = strike width minus net premium.
  • pnl — Total dollar P&L for this trade. Green = profit, Red = loss.
  • exit_reason — TAKE_PROFIT, STOP_LOSS, EXPIRATION, or EOD.
Time Metrics (2 columns)
  • dte — Days to Expiration at entry. 0 = same-day (0DTE).
  • dit — Days in Trade. For 0DTE, typically a fraction (e.g., 0.2 = ~5 hours).
Capital Tracking (2 columns)
  • capital_before — Account balance before this trade.
  • capital_after — Account balance after close. = capital_before + pnl.
Leg-Specific Columns (10 per leg)
Repeated for each leg: leg1_*, leg2_*, leg3_*, leg4_* (Iron Condors/Butterflies use 4 legs).
  • legN_symbol — Full OCC contract symbol (e.g., O:SPY260302P00682000).
  • legN_name — Leg name from config (e.g., "Short Put").
  • legN_strike — Strike price (e.g., 682.00).
  • legN_entry_price — Option premium at entry.
  • legN_exit_price — Option premium at exit.
  • legN_iv — Implied volatility at entry.
  • legN_delta — Delta at entry. Negative for puts, positive for calls.
  • legN_gamma — Gamma at entry. Rate of delta change.
  • legN_theta — Theta at entry. Daily time decay (negative = loses value).
  • legN_vega — Vega at entry. Sensitivity to 1% IV change.
A 2-leg strategy has 39 total columns. A 4-leg strategy (Iron Condor) has 59 columns. The Greeks columns (delta, gamma, theta, vega) are invaluable for understanding why specific trades won or lost.

Decision Tree Log Book

A chronological, day-by-day record of what the backtester did on each trading day. Shows not only which trades were taken, but also why trades were skipped. Each day is a collapsible row with a status badge (Entry/Exit/Skipped) and P&L. Click to expand and see the full decision flow.

Event Types
The expanded day view shows a vertical timeline of events. Each event has a distinct type, icon, and color:
NO DATA
No market data available for this day (weekend, holiday, or data gap).
CONDITIONS NOT MET
Entry conditions were checked but not satisfied. The reason shows the specific condition that failed, including the actual vs. required values:
  • Preset conditions — e.g., "Premarket Change 0.50% failed > 1%", "Gap -2.10% failed > 0%", "Change % 0.42% failed > 1.00%"
  • Custom conditions — e.g., "price 450.20 < SMA20 455.00", "RSI 45.2 failed < 30", "Missing RSI data"
  • Time window — e.g., "Conditions not met in range 10:00-10:30"
These specific failure reasons help you tune your conditions. If you see "Change % 0.42% failed > 1.00%" on many days, consider lowering your threshold.
CONDITIONS MET
All entry conditions passed. Shows the price and time when they were satisfied. The backtester will now attempt to enter a trade.
TRADE SKIPPED
Conditions were met but the trade could not be executed. The reason explains why:
  • Unable to fetch valid option data for strikes — No options contracts available at the target strikes/DTE.
  • No common timestamps across all option legs — Leg data doesn't align for a simultaneous fill.
  • Insufficient capital — Account balance too low for required margin.
  • PDT restriction — Pattern Day Trader rule blocking the trade.
  • Concurrent trade limit reached — Another position is already open.
  • Premium outside filter range — Net premium doesn't meet min/max filter.
  • IV entry condition not met — IV% filter threshold not satisfied (e.g., "IV 18.5% failed > 25%").
ENTRY
Trade successfully opened. Shows: number of contracts, net premium, max risk, expiration date, and per-leg details (position, type, strike, entry price).
EXIT
Trade closed. Shows trade number, exit time, exit premium, and P&L (color-coded). Exit reason sub-icons:
  • STOP_LOSS — Loss limit hit.
  • TAKE_PROFIT — Profit target reached.
  • EXPIRATION — Options expired.
  • EOD — Closed at market close.
ERROR
An error occurred during processing. Shows the error description (e.g., missing data, API timeout, invalid option chain).
Why the Decision Tree Matters
In most backtests, skipped days far outnumber traded days. The decision tree answers: "What happened on the days you didn't trade?"
  • Tune conditions — Too many "CONDITIONS NOT MET" days? Your thresholds may be too strict.
  • Debug data gaps — "NO DATA" entries reveal missing market data days.
  • Validate logic — Are entries triggered at the right conditions and times?
  • Understand skips — PDT, capital, or option data issues blocking trades?

Interpreting Your Results

Signs of a Good Strategy
  • Profit Factor > 1.3 — Earning $1.30+ for every $1 lost.
  • Max Drawdown < 20% — Worst period is manageable.
  • 30+ trades — Statistically significant sample size.
  • Smooth equity curve — Consistent returns, not a few lucky trades.
Red Flags
  • Profit Factor < 1.0 — Strategy loses money.
  • High win rate (>90%) + low profit factor — Small wins erased by rare large losses.
  • Fewer than 15 trades — Could be random chance.
  • Equity curve driven by 1-2 big trades — Not repeatable.
Backtest Limitations
  • Slippage — Real fills may be worse, especially for illiquid options.
  • Commissions — Not included. Factor $0.50-$1.00 per contract per leg.
  • Liquidity — Wide bid-ask spreads on illiquid options reduce real profits.
  • Regime changes — What worked in 2023 may not work in 2025.

Trade Log Columns (10 total)
  • trade_num — Sequential trade number (1, 2, 3...).
  • symbol — Ticker symbol traded (e.g., SPY). Especially useful in multi-symbol backtests.
  • entry_date — Date the trade was opened.
  • entry_price — Price per share at entry.
  • exit_date — Date the trade was closed.
  • exit_price — Price per share at exit.
  • shares — Number of shares traded.
  • pnl — Dollar P&L for the trade. Green = profit, Red = loss.
  • pnl_pct — Percentage P&L relative to entry price.
  • exit_reason — Why the trade closed: Take Profit, Stop Loss, Max Days, Signal Exit, or End of Data.

Decision Tree Log Book

A chronological, day-by-day record of what the backtester did on each trading day. Shows which trades were taken and why trades were skipped. Each day is a collapsible row. Click to expand and see the full decision flow.

Event Types
NO DATA
No market data available for this day (weekend, holiday, or data gap).
NO SIGNAL / CONDITIONS NOT MET
Entry conditions were checked but not satisfied. Shows the specific condition that failed:
  • Preset conditions — e.g., "Gap % 0.32% failed > 2.00%", "Premarket Change 0.8% failed > 2%"
  • Custom conditions — e.g., "No instance of Price > SMA(20)", "Prior conditions not met for: RSI < 30"
If most days show "NO SIGNAL", your entry conditions may be too strict. Try lowering the threshold.
CONDITION MET
Entry conditions passed. Shows the price, time, and condition values when the signal fired.
ENTRY
Trade opened. Shows: number of shares, entry price, and the triggering condition value (e.g., "100 shares @ $482.30 (Gap 3.15%)").
EXIT
Trade closed. Shows trade number, exit reason, and P&L. Exit reasons:
  • Take Profit — Profit target reached.
  • Stop Loss — Loss limit hit.
  • Max Days — Time limit reached.
  • Signal Exit — Exit condition triggered.
  • End of Data — Backtest period ended with position still open.
Why the Decision Tree Matters
  • Tune conditions — Too many "NO SIGNAL" days? Your thresholds may be too strict.
  • Debug data gaps — "NO DATA" entries reveal missing market data.
  • Validate logic — Are entries triggered at the right prices and conditions?
  • Understand exits — Are most exits from Stop Loss? Your SL may be too tight.

Interpreting Your Results

Signs of a Good Strategy
  • Profit Factor > 1.3 — Earning $1.30+ for every $1 lost.
  • Max Drawdown < 20% — Worst period is manageable.
  • 30+ trades — Statistically significant sample size.
  • Smooth equity curve — Consistent returns, not driven by a few lucky trades.
  • Multiple exit reasons — Healthy mix of TP, SL, and Max Days exits.
Red Flags
  • Profit Factor < 1.0 — Strategy loses money.
  • All exits are Stop Losses — Entry conditions or SL level needs adjustment.
  • Fewer than 15 trades — Could be random chance.
  • Equity curve driven by 1-2 big trades — Not repeatable.
Backtest Limitations
  • Slippage — Real fills may be worse, especially in fast-moving stocks.
  • Commissions — Not included. Factor in broker fees for accurate results.
  • Market impact — Large share sizes can move the market in real trading.
  • Regime changes — Past performance doesn't guarantee future results.

Simulated Trading Reference

Complete guide to setting up sessions, using the live trading interface, and interpreting your results

What is Simulated Trading?

Simulated Trading (also called Manual Backtesting or Paper Trading) lets you practice trading with virtual capital using real historical market data. Unlike the automated backtester, you make every trading decision — when to enter, when to exit, and how much to risk.

Why Use It
  • Practice without risk — Test strategies with $0 on the line.
  • Learn price action — Watch how candles form and patterns develop in real-time playback.
  • Build discipline — Practice entry/exit execution before risking real money.
  • Test options strategies — Execute complex multi-leg options trades with live Greeks and payoff diagrams.
  • Review and improve — Analyze your results with equity curves, trade logs, and performance metrics.
1/5

Symbol

Symbol Input
The ticker symbol of the stock or ETF you want to trade. Historical intraday (1-minute) bar data will be fetched for this symbol. Defaults to SPY.
Start with liquid, high-volume tickers like SPY, QQQ, or AAPL for the most realistic experience with minimal data gaps.
2/5

Date Range

Chart Start Date / Chart End Date
The full range of historical data to load into the chart. This determines how much history you can scroll back and see — the chart will contain all candles from this range.
Trading Start Date
The date where you will actually begin placing trades. The chart will start playback from this date, but you can scroll back to see earlier candles for context. Must fall between the Chart Start and Chart End dates.
Set the Chart Start Date a few weeks before your Trading Start Date. This gives you historical context (support/resistance levels, trends) before you start trading.
3/5

Trading Mode

Stock vs Options
Choose your trading mode. This affects the entire trading interface:
  • Stock Mode — Simple buy/sell with share quantities. You see a BUY/SELL panel with quantity input and a Close All button.
  • Options Mode — Full options trading with 22+ strategies, Greeks, payoff diagrams, TP/SL automation, and multi-leg configuration. A dedicated options panel appears below the stock controls.
Start with Stock mode to get familiar with the interface and chart controls. Switch to Options mode once you're comfortable with the playback system.
4/5

Account Balance

Starting Capital
The virtual account balance you start with. This amount decreases when you open positions and increases/decreases based on your P&L. Default is $100,000.
5/5

Start Trading

Clicking "Start Trading" loads the historical data for your symbol and date range, then opens the active trading interface. Your session is saved automatically — you can close the page and resume later from the session list.

Resuming Sessions
Active sessions appear in a list on the main Simulated Trading page. Each session card shows: symbol, trading mode, current balance, realized P&L, unrealized P&L, closed trade count, and a Resume button. Sessions persist in your browser until you end them.

Top Bar — Symbol, Price & Time

Back Button
Returns to the session setup page. Your session state is saved automatically — you can resume it anytime from the active sessions list.
Symbol & Price
Shows the current ticker symbol, the last closing price of the current candle, and the price change percentage from the previous candle.
Simulation Time
The current date and time in the simulation (Eastern Time). This advances as you step through bars or use autoplay. This is the "present" moment in the simulation — you're trading at this point in history.
Bar Visibility Info
Shows your position in the data (e.g., "Bar 150/390"). Helps you know how much data remains before the end of the session.

Timeframe Selectors

Available Timeframes
Switch between 7 timeframes to view candles at different granularities. The simulator fetches 1-minute data and aggregates it on the fly:
  • 1m — 1-minute candles. Highest resolution, best for scalping practice.
  • 5m / 15m / 30m — Intermediate timeframes for intraday patterns.
  • 1h / 2h — Higher timeframes for swing trade analysis.
  • 4h — Lowest resolution. Best for identifying major trends and support/resistance.
Use multi-timeframe analysis: check the 1h chart for the trend, then drop to 5m or 15m for precise entries. Switching timeframes does not affect your position or playback state.

Playback Controls

Prev Bar / Next Bar
Manually step backward or forward one bar at a time. Each step reveals the next candle on the chart (in the current timeframe). This is the most precise way to advance the simulation.
Play / Pause (Autoplay)
Toggle automatic bar advancement. When playing, bars advance at the configured Speed interval. Press again to pause. The icon toggles between Play (▶) and Pause (⏸).
Reset View
Centers the chart back to the current simulation candle. Useful after scrolling back in history to review past price action.
Interval / Speed / Skip
Three controls that govern autoplay behavior:
  • Interval — How many minutes of data to advance per step: 1 min, 5 min, 15 min, 30 min, or 1 hr. At "5 min" interval, each autoplay tick adds 5 minutes of candle data.
  • Speed — How fast the autoplay runs: 0.5s (fastest), 1s, 2s, 3s (default), or 5s (slowest) between each step.
  • Skip — Number of bars to jump per step (default: 1). Set to 5 to skip 5 bars at a time for faster advancement.
For quick review: set Interval to 30 min, Speed to 0.5s, Skip to 5. For careful analysis: use 1 min interval, manual step-by-step.

Jump to Date & Time

Go to Date / Time
Jump directly to a specific date and time in the simulation. Enter a date (MM/DD/YYYY) and optional time (HH:MM), then click the search button. The chart advances to that point, revealing all candles up to that moment. Useful for skipping weekends, overnight periods, or jumping to a specific market event.
To skip to market open: enter the date and type "09:30". To jump to close: type "16:00". Market hours are 9:30 AM - 4:00 PM ET.

Technical Indicators

Adding Indicators
Click "Indicators" in the toolbar to open the dropdown. Configure the indicator type, period, line thickness (1-4px), and color, then click "Add to Chart". Active indicators appear as overlay lines on the candlestick chart.
Indicator Types
  • SMA (Simple Moving Average) — Average price over the last N candles. Configurable period (e.g., 20, 50, 200). Good for identifying trends and dynamic support/resistance.
  • EMA (Exponential Moving Average) — Weighted moving average that reacts faster to recent price changes. More responsive than SMA for short-term trading.
  • VWAP (Volume Weighted Average Price) — Average price weighted by volume. Resets daily. Institutional benchmark — price above VWAP = bullish, below = bearish.
Managing Active Indicators
Active indicators are listed at the bottom of the dropdown with a remove button (×). Each shows the type, period, and color. You can add multiple indicators simultaneously (e.g., SMA 20 + SMA 50 for crossover strategies).

OHLC Data Bar

Candle Data
Displays the OHLCV data for the current or hovered candle. Updates in real-time as you move your mouse over the chart:
  • O — Open price (where the candle started)
  • H — High price (highest point, shown in green)
  • L — Low price (lowest point, shown in red)
  • C — Close price (where the candle ended)
  • Vol — Volume (number of shares traded in this candle)
  • IV — Implied Volatility (shown in purple, used for options pricing)

Interactive Chart

The main chart area uses TradingView's Lightweight Charts library. It displays candlestick data with volume bars at the bottom. The chart is interactive — you can scroll, zoom, and hover for details.

Chart Interactions
  • Scroll — Pan left/right through historical candles.
  • Pinch/Zoom — Zoom in for detail or out for the big picture.
  • Hover — Shows crosshair and updates the OHLC bar with that candle's data.
Visual Overlays
When you have an open position, the chart automatically shows:
  • Entry Price Line — A horizontal dashed line at your entry price, visible across all candles.
  • P&L Shading — Green shading above entry (profit zone) or red shading below entry (loss zone) for active positions.
  • Indicator Lines — SMA, EMA, and VWAP overlays if configured.

Stock Trading Panel

Account Metrics
Three real-time metrics displayed on the left side of the trading panel:
  • Balance — Your current available capital. Decreases when you open positions, adjusts on close.
  • Realized P&L — Total profit/loss from closed trades. Green = profit, red = loss.
  • Unrealized P&L — Current profit/loss on open positions (mark-to-market). Updates as the price moves.
BUY Button
Opens a long position at the current candle's closing price. Enter the number of shares in the Quantity input first. The cost (shares × price) is deducted from your balance.
SELL Button
Opens a short position at the current candle's closing price. You profit if the price goes down. Same quantity input as BUY.
CLOSE ALL
Closes all open stock positions at the current candle's closing price. Appears only when you have open positions. Realized P&L is updated immediately.

Options Trading Panel

The options panel appears below the stock controls when the session is in Options mode. It provides a complete options trading experience with strategy selection, leg configuration, Greeks, and automated TP/SL.

DTE (Days to Expiration)
Set the number of days until the option expires. Enter 0 for same-day (0DTE) options, or any value up to 365. The Black-Scholes model uses this along with IV, strike price, and underlying price to calculate the option's theoretical value and Greeks.
0DTE options have the fastest time decay (theta) and are highly sensitive to price moves. Great for practicing quick scalps.
Strategy Selector
Choose from 22 options strategies organized into 5 groups:
  • Single Leg — Long Call, Long Put, Naked Short Call, Naked Short Put
  • Vertical Spreads — Short/Long Put Spread, Short/Long Call Spread
  • Iron Strategies — Short/Long Iron Condor, Short/Long Iron Butterfly
  • Straddles & Strangles — Long/Short Straddle, Long/Short Strangle
  • Calendar & Diagonal — Calendar Call/Put Spread, Diagonal Call/Put Spread, Double Calendar, Double Diagonal
The strategy selection auto-configures the correct number of legs, strikes, and position types. You can fine-tune each leg in the Leg Configuration section below.
Take Profit (TP)
Optional auto-close when profit reaches a threshold. Set a value and choose the type:
  • % (Percentage) — Close when unrealized profit reaches X% of the total cost to open. Example: Cost is $1,000, TP is 35% → closes at $350 profit.
  • $ (Dollar) — Close when unrealized profit reaches a fixed dollar amount per contract.
Stop Loss (SL)
Optional auto-close when loss reaches a threshold. Works the same way as TP but in reverse:
  • % (Percentage) — Close when unrealized loss reaches X% of the cost to open. Example: Cost is $1,000, SL is 200% → closes at $2,000 loss.
  • $ (Dollar) — Close when unrealized loss reaches a fixed dollar amount per contract.
Quantity
Number of contracts to trade. Each contract represents 100 shares of the underlying stock. Default is 10.
Trade Button
Executes the options trade with the current configuration (DTE, strategy, legs, quantity). The position opens at the theoretical price calculated from Black-Scholes at the current simulation time. A confirmation toast appears showing the trade details.

Leg Configuration & Presets

For multi-leg strategies (spreads, condors, butterflies), the Leg Configuration section appears below the strategy selector. It shows each leg with its parameters and allows adjustment.

Per-Leg Parameters
Each leg displays and lets you configure:
  • Type — Call or Put
  • Position — Long (buy) or Short (sell)
  • Strike Price — Relative to the current price (e.g., ATM, OTM by $5)
  • DTE — Days to expiration (may differ for calendar/diagonal spreads)
Presets
Quick-apply common configurations for the selected strategy (e.g., "Narrow Width" vs "Wide Width" for spreads, "$2 OTM" vs "$5 OTM" for single legs). Click a preset button to automatically adjust all leg strikes.

Payoff Diagram Modal

When you have an open options position, click it to open the Payoff Diagram modal — a live, interactive visualization of your position's profit/loss profile.

P&L Curve
The main chart shows the theoretical P&L at different underlying prices. It plots: max profit, max loss, breakeven points, and your current position on the curve.
Position Stats
Above the chart, a stats bar shows: strategy name, current unrealized P&L, underlying price, net delta, theta, and time remaining.
Payoff Playback
The payoff modal has its own playback controls (Previous/Play/Next). You can step through bars within the modal and watch how the P&L curve shifts as time passes and the underlying price moves. Playback speed is configurable (1x, 2x, 4x, 10x).
Close Position
The "Close Position" button at the bottom right of the modal closes the options position at the current theoretical value. P&L is realized and the position is removed from your open positions list.

Open Positions List (Options)

When you have open options positions, an "Open Positions" card appears at the bottom of the options panel. It lists every active position with real-time P&L updates.

Position Card Info
Each position card shows: strategy name, quantity, net premium (entry cost), current value, unrealized P&L (with color coding), and Greeks (Delta, Gamma, Theta, Vega). Click a position to open its Payoff Diagram.
TP/SL Automation
If you set TP or SL when placing the trade, positions are automatically closed when the threshold is hit during playback. A notification toast confirms the auto-close with the realized P&L.

End Session

End Session Button
Closes all remaining open positions at the current price and finalizes the session. A confirmation dialog appears first. After ending:
  • All open stock and options positions are closed at current prices.
  • Final balance, P&L, and all trade data are saved.
  • You are redirected to the Results page with full performance analysis.
  • The session is removed from the "active sessions" list.
You don't have to end a session immediately. Use the Back button to save your progress and return later to resume trading from where you left off.

Results Overview

After ending a session, you are taken to the Results Detail page. This page provides a complete performance analysis of your trading session — everything you need to evaluate your decisions and improve.

Session Summary
The header shows: Session ID, Symbol, Total Trades, and Net P&L with a color-coded icon (green checkmark for profit, red X for loss).

Performance Statistics

All Metrics
  • Win Rate — Percentage of trades that were profitable.
  • Wins / Losses — Count of winning vs losing trades.
  • Profit Factor — Gross Profit / Gross Loss. Above 1.0 = profitable overall.
  • Max Drawdown — Largest peak-to-trough decline in portfolio value.
  • Sharpe Ratio — Risk-adjusted return. Higher = better risk/reward.
  • Gross Profit / Gross Loss — Total dollars won and lost.
  • Average Win / Average Loss — Mean dollar value of winning and losing trades.
  • Max Win / Max Loss — Largest single winning and losing trade.
  • Max Consecutive Wins / Losses — Longest winning and losing streaks.
  • Average Trade Duration — Mean time between entry and exit.
  • Initial Balance / Final Balance — Starting vs ending capital.
  • Net Return % — Overall percentage return on initial capital.

Equity Curve

Reading the Equity Curve
The equity curve plots your portfolio value after each closed trade. A dashed horizontal line marks your initial balance. Interactive tooltips show the exact dollar value at each trade point. Look for a smooth upward curve — jagged or declining curves indicate inconsistent or losing strategies.
